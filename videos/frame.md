# frame.md - the camera design system

The rules every composition in the Orleans.Lattice video series follows. The
series does not have a design of its own: it is the documentation site's design
system - the order diagram - translated for a 1920x1080 frame.

## Source of truth

- **The site's design system** is the source of every colour, typeface,
  diagram role and figure: `docs-site/template/public/tokens.css` (paper and
  board palettes, contrast-checked to WCAG 2.2 AA), the self-hosted fonts
  beside it, the mark, and the join-figure scenarios in
  `docs-site/figures/join-figures.json`, all documented in `DESIGN.md` at the
  repository root.
- **Compositions read it, never copy it.** `tools/hf.js` copies it into
  `assets/brand/site/` (ignored by git) before every preview, check and render,
  and [assets/brand/brand.css](assets/brand/brand.css) imports that copy.
  Compositions use the site's tokens by the site's names (`--lt-*`), so a
  change to the site's design reaches the videos on the next render. If the
  tokens are missing, the render stops rather than falling back.
- **What this file owns** is what a web page cannot supply: the canvas, the
  camera type scale (`--lv-*`), and how the site's figures move on screen.

## Canvas

- 1920x1080 at 30 fps, 16:9 landscape.
- **Paper by default**, as on the site: ink on white, as in a printed
  mathematics text. **Board** - chalk on a green-grey slate - is the site's dark
  material; a composition, or one scene in it, uses it with
  `data-bs-theme="dark"`.
- Title-safe area: 5% of each edge (96 px left and right, 54 px top and
  bottom). No text outside it.
- Caption band: the bottom 18% of the frame is kept clear of essential content
  so captions never cover it.

## Type

One proportional family carries every voice and weight marks importance, as on
the site; code and state labels alone take the monospace face.

- **Families:** `var(--lv-font-sans)` and `var(--lv-font-mono)`. They are the
  site's own self-hosted families (Recursive Sans Linear and Lattice Mono, its
  Cascadia Mono), generated by the sync with only a generic fallback: the
  site's stacks go on to name system and web fonts, and the renderer would
  fetch those from the network.
- **Weights:** the site's `--lt-weight-*` tokens (body, label, strong, heading,
  display).
- **Sizes**, in pixels at 1080p (the `--lv-type-*` tokens):

| Step | Size | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hero | 112 | the title card, once per episode |
| title | 80 | a scene title |
| heading | 56 | a diagram or panel heading |
| body | 44 | a short on-screen statement |
| code | 36 | code panels |
| state | 34 | a state label in a diagram (`A=3, B=5`) |
| caption | 36 | captions |
| label | 32 | diagram notes and edge labels, kickers; the smallest text ever shown |

- Nothing below 32 px. Text smaller than that cannot be read in motion on a
  laptop at normal distance.
- At most about twelve words of prose on screen at once. The narration carries
  the argument; on-screen text anchors it.
- Sentence case for titles and headings.
- Terms exactly as the corpus uses them: tree, shard, leaf, write-ahead log
  (WAL), seam, companion package, facade, silo, grain, estate, region, lattice
  or CRDT primitive, `ILattice`.

## Colour

The site's roles, by the site's names. Colour carries meaning, never
decoration.

| Role | Token | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| surface | `--lt-surface`, `--lt-surface-sunken` | the ground; code and quiet panels |
| ink | `--lt-ink`, `--lt-ink-2`, `--lt-ink-3` | text, then secondary text, then notes and edge labels |
| rules | `--lt-rule`, `--lt-rule-strong` | hairlines; the pale order behind a figure |
| link | `--lt-link` | a reference, as a paper sets its links in blue ink |
| marker | `--lt-marker`, `--lt-marker-soft`, `--lt-marker-ink` | **the join, and "you are here" only** |
| semantic | `--lt-info`, `--lt-warning`, `--lt-danger`, `--lt-success` | callouts |
| syntax | `--lt-syn-*` | code |

- The marker is the only accent with a structural role. It marks the join and
  the viewer's current position (the current episode on a path, the current
  step of a sequence), and nothing else - not titles, not emphasis.
- On paper the marker is too pale to stand alone: pair it with an ink ring or
  ink text (`--lt-marker-ink`), as the site does.
- Every text pair passes WCAG 2.2 AA as measured by `npm run check`, which
  fails the build otherwise. Colour is never the only carrier of a state.

## Diagram vocabulary

The series draws in the site's notation: order diagrams (Hasse diagrams), which
is also where the project's name comes from.

- A **state** is a node. **Order runs upwards**: an edge from a lower node to a
  higher one means the higher state includes the lower.
- The **bottom** element - a shared starting state, or an unreleased package -
  is a hollow node (`--lt-diagram-node-hollow`, ringed in ink).
- **Concurrent** states - writes neither of which is above the other - sit
  level, in `--lt-diagram-concurrent`, joined by a dashed line labelled
  "concurrent".
- The **join** - the least state above both, the merged state - is the one
  node in the marker (`--lt-diagram-join`, ringed with `--lt-diagram-join-ring`).
- **Edges** are the covering relation, drawn upward: pale
  (`--lt-diagram-edge`) as the order, in ink (`--lt-diagram-edge-active`) as
  they are traversed.
- A **chain** - one replica's history, or a reader's path - is a vertical run
  of nodes, with the marker on the current one.
- **State labels** are in the monospace face, in the notation the CRDT guide
  uses (`A=3, B=5`), with a note beneath in the proportional face (`join,
  value 8`).

### The join figure

The site's canonical animation - on its home page and on every CRDT explainer
page - is the series' signature figure:
[compositions/components/join-diagram.html](compositions/components/join-diagram.html).
It is the site's figure at twice its size, and it moves as the site's does:

1. The order is on screen first: pale edges, the hollow bottom, the two
   concurrent writes and their labels. The join is hollow.
2. The lower edges draw in ink (1.3 s), then the upper edges (1.3 s).
3. Meanwhile a delta travels from the bottom state through each concurrent
   write to the join (2.6 s), visible between 6% and 94% of the way.
4. When both arrive, the join fills with the marker, its label appears, and it
   pulses once (0.9 s).
5. Optionally, one delta is delivered again (1.4 s) and nothing changes: the
   join already holds it. That is idempotence, shown rather than stated.

Its labels are the site's own: a composition names a scenario from
`docs-site/figures/join-figures.json` (`"scenario": "pncounter"`) and the figure
reads its node states, notes and edge labels, and which write is re-delivered,
from there, so a figure here always says what the site's figure for that CRDT
says. The camera shows the site's "concurrent" annotation as its term only, and
leaves the gloss to the narration; each node's labels are centred on the node,
so a two-line state stays inside the frame. The diamond layout is ported. The
site's two chain layouts - comparable writes, where the join is the higher
write (Max- and Min-Register), and a write that changes nothing (OR-Set,
OR-Flag) - are ported with the first episode that needs them, and until then a
chain scenario fails the render rather than being drawn wrongly.

Build any other figure the same way: show the order first, then move along it.
Hold a finished figure for at least a second and a half, and keep to one
figure idea per scene.

System diagrams (silo, grain, shard, write-ahead log, region) use the same
roles and stroke weights. Each is added as a shared component with the first
episode that needs it, not ahead of time.

## Code on screen

- Code shown in a video comes only from the episode's companion page,
  `docs/videos/<episode>.md`, where it is a column-0 ` ```csharp verify ` fence
  with a `<!-- video-snippet: <id> -->` marker directly above it. The
  repository's Roslyn harness compiles it; `npm run snippets` copies it into
  the composition (`<code data-snippet="<id>">`), and `npm run snippets:check`
  fails CI if the two drift. What is on screen is what compiled.
- Code panels use the site's code ground (`--lt-code-bg`, `--lt-code-ink`) and
  syntax roles (`--lt-syn-*`), in the code type step, at most twelve lines.
  Mark the line the narration is on with the marker's soft band
  (`--lt-marker-soft`) - it is where the viewer is.
- Reveal code by line or by highlight. Never type it out character by character
  for more than a single line.

## Motion

- Motion explains; it never decorates. Every movement answers "what changed?".
- **Eases** come from the site through
  [assets/brand/motion.js](assets/brand/motion.js): `lattice-out` is the site's
  `--lt-ease-out`, for entrances and state changes; `lattice-draw` is the join
  figure's curve, for anything drawn or travelling along an order.
- **Durations** are the site's `--lt-duration-*` tokens
  (`LatticeMotion.duration`), and the figure's choreography is
  `LatticeMotion.figure`. A composition that needs a new timing adds it there,
  once.
- One primary movement at a time; anything secondary is subordinate and slower.
- Time visual changes to the narration cue timeline
  (`renders/narration/<slug>/cues.json`), never to guessed durations.
- Deterministic by construction: paused GSAP timelines, colours read from the
  tokens once and tweened as values (never CSS transitions), no wall clock, no
  unseeded randomness, no network at render time.

## Captions

- Every episode ships captions (`narration.vtt`) in the written form. The
  pronunciation lexicon changes only what the voice says, never what is shown.
- Captions are delivered as a WebVTT track the viewer can switch off, rather
  than burned into the picture, wherever the player supports it.
- At most two lines of 42 characters.

## Audio

- Narration is the only required layer, in the series voice
  ([voice/voice.json](voice/voice.json)).
- Music is optional and off by default. When used it sits under the narration
  and never carries meaning.
- Sound effects only for events the narration names.
- Loudness: -16 LUFS integrated, true peak below -1 dBTP, consistent across
  episodes.

## Honest status

- A package that is unreleased or in progress is labelled on screen wherever it
  appears; in a diagram it is a hollow node, as on the site.
- No claim the corpus does not make, and no invented customers, adoption
  figures or benchmark numbers. A guarantee is stated as precisely as the
  documentation states it, with the test, specification or measurement behind
  it.
